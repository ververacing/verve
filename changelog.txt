Verve - changelog

0.10.5
- Recovered cars no longer set off backwards. Putting a car back on the racing line sets its orientation
  but not its speed, so a car sliding out of a crash kept its backward momentum -- it would land facing
  forward but drift the wrong way, fight itself, and beach in the grass. It's now given a small forward
  push so it sets off the right way and just drives on.
- Repeat-offender retirements: a car that's been crash-repaired many times and keeps wrecking itself is
  a broken car -- it now retires instead of crash-looping all race and finishing laps down. This brings
  retirements up from near-zero to a realistic handful, and self-scales -- a clean track barely triggers
  it, a crash-heavy one retires its worst few. (It also cuts the total repair churn a lot, since the
  hopeless cars stop generating endless repairs.)
- Cleaner behaviour when you're being lapped: a lapped car now eases aside DECISIVELY and keeps rolling
  instead of stacking every caution into a near-stop -- which was making faster cars pile up and rear-end
  it. It moves clearly off-line, holds that line (no swerving for other obstacles while it yields), and
  only lifts a touch as the car actually draws alongside.
- No more giant berth for a car that's already off the road: cars only sweep around an obstacle that's
  actually on the racing surface. A car parked well off to the side gets passed on the line, no swerve.

0.10.4
- Pile-ups no longer wipe out the grid. When a knot of cars gets wedged together and can't be freed,
  each one now gets one rescue -- a fresh body and a force-placed return to the racing line at a
  STAGGERED point, so the heap lands strung out over a stretch of track instead of restacking -- then
  keeps being protected while it drives away. Cars in a heap get separated and rejoin, rather than all
  timing out together and retiring at once.
- "Limp repairs" no longer runs away. The give-a-hobbled-car-a-fresh-body helper now re-bodies a given
  car at most ONCE per damage episode (it was re-firing every few seconds on kerb-worn cars, showing
  hundreds of "repairs" a race). Body damage only, since that's all it can actually fix.
- Retirement count is now the real thing: it counts cars that ACTUALLY retired, not our own "give up"
  decisions -- so the number you see matches the grid (and the ~10.5% real-world target).
- Trouble-spot learning is protected from being wiped. A reload used to be able to overwrite a track's
  learned hot-spots with an empty set; now learned data is never overwritten with nothing, so the
  adaptive crash-damping keeps building across sessions instead of resetting.

0.10.3
- Pick a driver for EVERY racer, not just per car model. The driver list is now one row per grid slot
  (numbered, with each racer's name), so a grid of identical cars can be a full grid of different
  drivers. Car class (the physics setting) stays per-model in its own section.
- Adaptive crash damping: Verve now uses a track's own learned trouble-spot history to tell how
  crash-prone it is, and calms the WHOLE field to suit -- more caution, less aggression, a bigger
  opening-lap ease, and the pack pace-stretch pulled back. A nasty track (Zandvoort) settles down on
  its own while a clean one stays racy. This attacks the crash RATE, not just after-the-fact repairs.
  Biggest effect on opening-lap pile-ups. (Needs Trouble-spot learning on.)
- Clearer status readout: the old "repaired & rejoined" tally is split into Crash repairs, Limp
  repairs, and Retired (genuinely wrecked) -- so you can see the real picture (retirements vs the
  ~10.5% real-world target) instead of one big, alarming number.
- Damaged cars limp for far less time: the "give a knocked-about car a fresh wing/body" trigger now
  catches smaller aero knocks and acts sooner, so a hobbled car rejoins racing pace instead of
  crawling round for laps holding everyone up.
- Cars go around a genuinely slow car instead of piling into it: the "take the open road" move now
  also fires for a car limping at 30-55 km/h (not only a nearly-stopped one) -- including YOUR car if
  you're wounded -- so fast traffic sweeps past cleanly instead of queuing up and rear-ending it. Tuned
  so it does NOT fire on a car merely braking for a corner (that was causing swerves in the braking zones).
- Fewer last-few-laps crashes: as tyres fade late in a stint, cars now back off a little more and
  drive within the worn grip, instead of running at the limit and sliding off at the end.

0.10.2
- Crash recovery no longer strands cars off-track. A beached car is only counted "repaired & rejoined"
  once it's actually been put back on the racing line -- and the reposition now only waits for a
  genuinely-close or fast-approaching car (not any car within ~40 m), so it succeeds instead of
  stalling forever. As an absolute backstop, a car that still can't be freed is force-placed back on
  the line rather than abandoned in the gravel for the whole race. (Fixes "Recovering right now" and
  the repaired count sticking, with cars sat off-track.)
- Limping cars cleared: a damaged car crawling on-track and holding up the field now gets a fresh
  body after a short grace, so it rejoins racing pace instead of dragging a train behind it for laps.
- Take the open road: if a stopped or crawling car is sitting on the track just ahead, cars now sweep
  around it on the OPEN side (a car stopped on the right sends the field left) instead of queuing up
  behind it. Cars already crawling in a queue behind the obstacle peel off and filter past too, and
  they key off the genuinely-stalled car rather than whoever's merely nearest -- so a crash clears
  instead of stacking the field nose-to-tail.
- Quicker to pounce on open space: a fast car behind a slower one now starts moving for the pass as
  soon as it has a real speed run, rather than waiting until it's almost touching -- and a genuine run
  resists the pack damping, so it can still pull into clear track in a crowd (most visible off the start).
- Less pack bunching: a car with clear road ahead now drives away on the racing line instead of running
  a slow defensive line every corner just because someone's on its tail -- so the front of a bunch
  stops anchoring the field. Leading a pack also earns a small pace stretch to string it out.
- Recovery powers out of gravel: a car driving itself back from an off now uses near-full throttle on
  soft ground instead of bogging down and stopping halfway (with the same firm reposition as a backstop
  if it still can't make it), so it reaches the tarmac and rejoins.
- Driver pace now genuinely strings the field out. The fastest driver ACTUALLY on the grid runs at the
  difficulty slider, and everyone else is spaced below by how far their pace rating trails his -- and
  that spread was widened, because the old amount barely separated cars (a mid-pack driver sat only a
  few hundredths of an AI level off the ace, so a varied grid still bunched). A randomized grid now
  spreads out properly instead of circulating nose-to-tail. (Aggression slider is still ignored for
  profiled cars; each drives to their own known aggression.)
- Starts eased slightly: the opening-lap caution was a touch too strong, so the field was over-cautious
  off the line. Trimmed and released a bit sooner (the grid funnel still spaces the merge to turn 1).

0.10.1
- Verve now controls RETIREMENTS, not AC. A car that crashes is held (AC's own retirement blocked),
  given a fresh wing/body in place after a penalty, and put back on the racing line if it's beached
  off-track -- so it rejoins and races on. Only genuinely wrecked cars (a big impact or broken
  suspension) or truly unrecoverable ones retire, so retirement rates land near real-world instead
  of losing half the grid. No teleporting into the pack, no pit-lane stranding. ("Crash repair",
  needs Self-recovery; off by default.)
- Trouble-spot learning: Verve learns where cars repeatedly crash on a track and adds a little
  caution there, so the field stops piling into the same corner. Per track and per car class, and
  REMEMBERED across sessions -- race #2 on a track already knows its hot spots. Self-corrects as a
  corner calms. ("Trouble-spot learning"; off by default.)
- Oval / NASCAR: a new stock-car class (huge draft, packs up in dirty air, uses the whole width),
  automatic oval-vs-road-course detection, and groove racing -- on a speedway, stock cars commit to
  a high or low lane and run side-by-side. Plus 80+ real NASCAR and oval drivers added to the driver
  profiles (Petty, Earnhardt, Larson, Hamlin...) and Indy oval aces.
- Cleaner racing: a grid funnel so the field merges onto the line gradually off the start instead of
  all diving for it at once; a launch boost so cars don't bog off the line; opportunistic inside AND
  outside passes that use more of the track when a car has a real speed run -- and scale with the
  driver's aggression (Verstappen takes every opening, Prost picks his); and an anti-rear-end ease so
  a fast-closing car doesn't pile into the back of a slower one in the braking zone, without killing
  the overtake.

0.10.0
- Driver profiles (optional, big one): assign any grid slot a real driver -- 300+ across F1,
  Le Mans/prototypes, GT, touring, rally, vintage, karts, IndyCar and drift -- or a generic
  archetype. Each has its own pace, aggression, risk and consistency drawn from public racing
  record, so the field feels like individuals. Class-filtered, type-to-search dropdown next to
  each car; one-click "Randomize driver grid"; session-only, so five identical cars can be five
  different drivers. Cross-discipline: drivers show up in every category they genuinely raced
  (e.g. Verstappen in a kart). Pace sets each car's AI level. Leave a slot blank for the normal
  slider-driven behaviour.
- Crash & retirement control: Verve now decides retirements, not AC. A car that spins or crashes
  is held (AC's own retirement blocked), given a quick wing/body repair in place after a penalty,
  and driven back out by self-recovery. Only genuinely wrecked cars (a big impact or broken
  suspension) or truly unrecoverable ones retire -- so an off costs time, not your race, and
  retirement rates land near real-world instead of losing half the grid. No teleporting, no
  pit-lane stranding. Toggle "Crash repair" (needs Self-recovery on); off by default.
- Race awareness: calmer, more spaced opening lap (far fewer first-corner pile-ups); cars ease
  off when there's nothing to race for; clean blue-flag behaviour (move aside and lift only as
  the faster car arrives -- otherwise keep racing); and "leave room" so an overlapping car isn't
  pinched into the wall.
- Ctrl+C takeover: when the AI drives your car, it now gets the full Verve treatment -- pace,
  racecraft and self-recovery -- and stays hands-off the instant you take back control.

0.9.0
- Human mistake variety: instead of one generic wobble, cars now make distinct, mostly
  recoverable errors -- a missed apex, a wide exit, a caught twitch, or a brief brake lockup
  under pressure. Most come with a small lift so a bobble self-corrects instead of sliding off.
- Move commitment: once a car commits to a pass or a defence it holds that intent for a beat
  instead of re-deciding every frame, so racecraft reads as decisive rather than hesitant.

0.8.0
- Three new car classes, all auto-detected and overridable per car:
  - Kart: no downforce, instant tyre warm-up, bumper-to-bumper slipstream racing.
  - Rally: AWD, strong in the wet, slidey but catchable (races like a grippy road car on tarmac).
  - Formula Jr (low-downforce open-wheelers, e.g. Formula Ford/Vee): detected by wing count, so
    they race in close slipstream packs instead of hanging back like a downforce F1.

0.7.3
- Class-profile QA pass: moderated the Touring class (it was near-max on line-swings,
  inside dives, and close-following all at once, which got chaotic) to aggressive-but-clean;
  lowered Vintage's mistake rate (classic cars should be forgiving, not error-prone). All
  other classes verified realistic and consistent.

0.7.2
- Less corner bunching: cars no longer 'pounce' to close a gap while braking into a corner
  (only on straights/fast sections), so packs don't compress in the braking zones.

0.7.1
- Big cut to attrition in long races (esp. Formula): dirty air now makes a following car BACK
  OFF (keep distance) instead of losing grip and sliding off through corners -- that grip loss
  was crashing fragile cars over a long stint. And high-downforce cars no longer "pounce" into
  gaps (they keep aero distance), reducing contact.

0.7.0
- Simpler control: removed the per-car Chill/Clean/Intense levels. The Difficulty (pace) and
  Aggression sliders cover how fast / how hard the field races, and the Variability slider now
  also spreads per-driver aggression, so the grid isn't uniform -- no manual per-car setup.
- Pounce: cars stay eager to fill a gap for a moment after following someone, taking advantage
  when space opens instead of hanging back. Scales with racecraft intensity.
- The per-car list is now just class detection/override (the physics knob).

0.6.9
- Fewer Formula/aero retirements: high-downforce cars keep more racing distance (they can't
  follow closely in dirty air anyway), reducing contact in big fragile fields.
- Late-race corners: halved the end-of-stint grip fade so it no longer stacks with AC's own
  tyre wear and drag the AI under-gripped into run-wides.

0.6.8
- Packs no longer crawl/concertina to a halt before corners: the pack-density damping now only
  calms the line-changing (so starts stay tidy) and no longer makes cars hang back in traffic,
  so they stay opportunistic and keep the field flowing.

0.6.7
- Track-edge safety: Verve no longer pushes a car further toward an edge it's already near,
  so it won't shove a car onto a kerb at a corner exit (fixes a near-rollover).
- Calmer race starts and packs (stronger pack-density damping); smaller pass/defend offsets.

0.6.6
- Base AI grip slider opened to 1.50 (above 1.20 = extra stick + speed).

0.6.5
- Base AI grip default returned to 1.20 (AC stock; fixes AI running wide at hard corners).

0.6.4
- Pack-density damping: cars hold formation in a crowd instead of all passing/defending at
  once (much calmer race starts and traffic).

0.6.3
- Aggression: racecraft now uses the car's own aggression (the Quick Race slider) as a
  baseline instead of overwriting it, and only adds a bit when fighting.
- Calmer racecraft (bigger deadzone, longer side-hold, smaller offsets).
- Cold-tyre corners handled better (esp. classic/muscle cars).
- Formula DRS discipline: closes DRS outside a zone / out of range; in-zone DRS left to the game.
- Per-car list split into "in this session" vs "saved overrides for other cars".

0.6.2
- Gentler racecraft for high-downforce cars (they keep more distance in dirty air).
- Traffic-aware recovery: a spun car waits for a gap instead of rejoining into oncoming cars.
- Faster recovery return (accelerates once pointed forward, hands back to the AI sooner).

0.6.1

- De-skittish defending (hold the inside line into corners; don't mirror/weave).
